Privacy

Your data, only what we need.

388 Pueblos is an open, free project. For the beta, we only store what is strictly necessary to keep you informed about the project. We do not sell your data or pass it on to anyone, and we do not use advertising or tracking cookies.

Who is responsible

388 Pueblos, a project by Ana Pérez Toledo (Guareña, Badajoz).
Contact for any privacy matter: to be published.

What data we store and why

  • Waiting list: your email and, if you enter them, your alias, your town and your profile (player, creator, town council…). We use them only to let you know about the beta, about news and about when your town wakes up.
  • Votes to wake towns up: the town you vote for, linked to a random identifier of your device (not to your name or your email). It is used to count one vote per person.
  • Stamp passport: stored only on your device (your browser’s local storage). It is not sent to any server.

The legal basis is your consent, which you give by ticking the box on the form and which you can withdraw at any time.

In the game

You do not need an account or an email to play. This is what happens to your data while you play:

  • Playing with others: your game sends our server your player name (a random one or one you choose, with two digits), where you are inside the game town, what you are doing and, if you use one, what your character from the editor looks like. The server passes this on to whoever is playing at that moment and forgets it when you leave: it does not store it. The website shows how many people are playing and which street of the game each one is in.
  • What you write to other players appears in a speech bubble at that moment and we do not store it. Do not write personal data about yourself or anyone else: anyone who is playing can read it.
  • Muting and reporting: your game creates a random number for your device; if you are reported, that number cannot speak for 15 minutes. It is not linked to your name or your email. The server writes each report, with the player name, in its technical log so it can be reviewed.
  • Meeting someone: the link carries a random code. Our server uses it while you play to place whoever opens it next to you, and does not pass it on to anyone.
  • Talking to the townspeople in the game: what you write to them goes to our server and, from there, to OpenAI (in the US), which answers as the townsperson. We ask it not to store it. If our server cannot answer, on a computer an AI that runs inside your browser may answer instead: the first time it is downloaded from Hugging Face (which receives your IP) and, from then on, what you write does not leave your device. If you enter your own OpenAI key, what you write goes straight to OpenAI with your key.
  • Sending us your feedback (the “Opinión” button or the O key): only if you press “Enviar” (Send), we receive a screenshot of what the game was showing, what you type, your voice if you record it (up to one minute), the name you add if you wish, the spot of the game town where you were, the in-game time, your screen size and which browser, system and graphics type you use. It goes to our server, which does not store it (it only remembers your IP until the end of the day, so nobody can send too many): it turns the voice into text with OpenAI (in the US) and delivers it to a private Discord channel (Discord Inc., in the US) that only the team can see. It stays there until we delete it; if you want yours deleted, write to us. We use it only to fix and improve the game. The microphone is requested only when you press “Grabar voz” (Record voice). Do not share personal data, yours or anyone else's.
  • The weather: the game asks Open-Meteo for the weather in the town. Only the town's position is sent; Open-Meteo receives your IP, as any website does.
  • In your browser (local storage, never sent to any server): your player name, the place where you left off (to go back there), your character from the editor, who you have muted, your code for meeting someone, your device number and, if you enter it, your OpenAI key. It is deleted when you clear this site's data in your browser.

The game's data is not based on your consent (except the feedback you send us, which only leaves if you send it) but on it being necessary to give you the game you are using and, for reporting and muting, on our legitimate interest in people being able to play respectfully.

Minors

If you are under 14, your mother, father or legal guardian must sign you up (article 7 of Spanish Organic Law 3/2018 on data protection, LOPDGDD). In the game you talk to people you do not know: if you are under 14, play with their permission.

Where it is stored

The waiting list and the votes are stored on our server, hosted on Render, and the game talks to that same server. Render (with the Cloudflare network in front of it) and OpenAI, for what is written to the townspeople in the game, act as data processors and may process data outside the European Economic Area under the safeguards of the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

Fonts and some libraries are loaded from jsDelivr and cdnjs, and the Guareña game is served from GitHub Pages; those services receive your IP address when they are downloaded, as with any website. We do not use any Google service.

How long we keep it

Until you ask us to delete it or the project ceases to exist. If you unsubscribe, we delete your email from the list.

Your rights

You can request access to, rectification or erasure of your data, restriction of or objection to its processing, and its portability, by writing to the contact above. If you believe we have not dealt with them properly, you can lodge a complaint with the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) (aepd.es).

The rules for playing with others and who is behind 388 Pueblos are in the legal notice.
